What are these background processes?

A process is a running program; threads are workers inside it. Game descendants are linked through parent process IDs. Other rows share the game's Android app account and may be launchers, Wine helpers, or another container; their exact relationship is unconfirmed. Names can be shortened by Android. CPU cores show average work between samples. Sleeping is often normal; a blocked state alone does not prove a problem. An exited zombie is waiting to be collected by its parent and is not treated as active CPU work.

Use this view to notice launchers or helpers doing work during a slowdown. It does not automatically stop processes. Whole-phone background competition remains available in the deep capture.

Other processes using CPU

CPU % is one thread's share of capture wall time. Runnable wait means ready to run but waiting for CPU; it excludes sleep and uncompleted waits. These costs overlap the game's total CPU work and do not measure total FEX/Wine overhead or GPU execution.

Learn to read threads, heat, and frame smoothness

Running: doing CPU work. Runnable: ready but waiting for CPU access. Sleeping or blocked: waiting for a timer, event, or other work. Runnable wait is different from sleep; use the deep capture to measure it.

Frame time: at 60 FPS the budget is about 16.7 ms per frame; at 30 FPS it is about 33.3 ms. Spikes can make motion uneven even with a good average FPS. The app HUD measures app-observed presents; SurfaceFlinger measures compositor updates.

Heat and clocks: compare temperatures and allowed CPU frequencies over a repeatable run. A lower frequency can reflect power policy or heat; it does not by itself prove thermal throttling.

Layer measurements: named DXVK or Wine workers describe parts of CPU work and overlap whole-game CPU totals. Do not add them together as a frame budget. Exact FEX, Wine, graphics-driver, and GPU execution times remain unavailable.
